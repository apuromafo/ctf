# Cyber Security Training for Your Non-Technical Team

**Cyber criminals exploit networks in search of vulnerabilities, even if that may be your workforce. Cyber security shouldn’t solely be the responsibility of a cyber team, but of all departments, including non-technical teams.**Whilst your staff are your greatest asset, they can also be the biggest threat in regard to cyber security. Ensuring all departments have an understanding of cyber security threats and how best to avoid and mitigate them should be key to the safety and security of your business. Having better cyber security awareness can, in many cases, prevent these threats from taking place.
## Who Needs Cyber Security Training?
[The cost of human error](https://tryhackme.com/resources/blog/cyber-security-the-cost-of-human-error?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) poses the largest cyber vulnerability to businesses, with most incidents attributable to human negligence and lack of cyber security awareness.Common [repercussions of a security breach](https://tryhackme.com/resources/blog/businesses-investing-in-cyber-security?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) frequently include loss of revenue, loss of intellectual property, damage to reputation, operational disruption, the cost of getting back up and running again, and loss of consumer trust.Understanding cyber security in the workplace is the first step in protecting your business, followed by implementing adequate security measures and ensuring all members of staff are educated on data security. Human error is known to be the biggest cause of cyber attacks, therefore investing in cyber security awareness training is vital.Cyber security awareness can be invaluable in arming employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent threats and reduce implications, which is why all workforces can benefit from cyber training. To varying degrees, all staff with access to data, networks and applications should be aware of risks and mitigations in ensuring resilient cyber defence and protection.We often speak about [building a team-wide cyber culture](https://tryhackme.com/resources/blog/how-to-build-a-cyber-culture-in-your-workforce?ref=blog.tryhackme.com), where employees feel empowered to learn and work within security practices. In addition to cyber awareness, it’s vital that all departments feel confident detecting and reporting potential threats, despite [39%](https://www.tessian.com/resources/security-cultures-report-2022/?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) of employees admitting they are unlikely to report a security incident.
## Why Cyber Security Training for Your Non-Cyber Team is Important
Employees play an essential role in maintaining security, therefore employees outside of a cyber team can benefit from cyber security training. Between phishing, social engineering attacks, malware distribution, password theft and breaches, there has never been a greater need for non-technical employees to maintain cyber security in the complex and dynamic landscape of today.In a recent study, [68%](https://www.morphisec.com/hubfs/2020%20State%20of%20Endpoint%20Security%20Final.pdf?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) of organisations experienced one or more endpoint attacks that successfully compromised data and/or their IT infrastructure, meanwhile, [44%](https://purplesec.us/resources/cyber-security-statistics/?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) of IT departments were found to manage somewhere between 5,000 and 500,000 endpoints.This highlights the importance of cyber security awareness training for employees, including software engineers and those working in IT and infrastructure who are frequently targeted by cyber criminals. Even for employees working outside of cyber security teams, having awareness of threats helps to amp up business defences for heightened protection. Naturally, some internal departments are less technical than cyber security employees, so an easy-to-follow, enjoyable mode of training that employees can complete in their own time is key.
