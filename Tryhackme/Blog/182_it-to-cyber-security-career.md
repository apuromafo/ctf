## A career in IT VS cyber security - which is best for me?
While careers in IT offer diverse opportunities, a path in cyber security presents a unique blend of challenge, intrigue, and crucial significance! To break both careers down, here is a comparison:
## General comparison
While IT professionals play a crucial role in maintaining technological infrastructure and supporting operations, their focus is primarily on ensuring systems run smoothly. On the other hand, cyber security professionals are the guardians of digital fortresses, tirelessly defending against malicious actors seeking to breach defences, steal sensitive data, or disrupt operations! The impact of cyber security professionals extends far beyond individual organisations, shaping the security landscape of entire industries and safeguarding the digital economy.
## Progression
Career progression in IT can be fantastic but may require navigating a crowded field of candidates competing for the same advancement opportunities. Progression in cyber security can be more promising with the right mix of skills, experience, and certifications, and with cyber professionals in **very** high demand at every level.
## Salaries
IT professionals command respectable salaries, with entry-level positions typically offering competitive pay. However, you must generally transition into management roles or specialise in high-demand areas (such as cloud computing, for example) to earn higher salaries and growth potential. In contrast, cyber security professionals enjoy some of the most lucrative salaries and consistently rank among the top earners in the tech industry! The specialised nature of cyber security roles, coupled with the ever-increasing demand for skilled professionals, results in premium compensation packages. In IT, entry-level roles (like help desk support or technical support) may have starting salaries ranging from $40,000 to $60,000 per year, depending on the location and company. As a comparison, entry-level roles in cyber security (such as Security Analyst or Junior Penetration Tester) may start around $60,000 to $80,000 annually.
## Career satisfaction
And in terms of general satisfaction, it’s ultimately down to your personal preference and interests! IT roles often involve problem-solving and troubleshooting. Similarly, in cyber security, every day presents a new puzzle to solve, a new threat to counter. The adrenaline rush of uncovering vulnerabilities, outsmarting hackers, and thwarting cyber attacks keeps professionals on the edge of their seats, knowing that their expertise directly thwarts digital adversaries.
## Demand for skills
Finally, on a very important note, the demand for cyber security professionals has reached unprecedented levels, with an estimated 3.5 million cyber professionals needed to meet the growing demand. Organisations worldwide are actively seeking skilled cyber security professionals to join the ranks and fortify their defences. Similarly to many IT jobs, the cyber security field offers solid job security!
## What is it like to work in cyber security?
A career in cyber security is dynamic and highly rewarding. Cyber security professionals play a crucial role in protecting organisations' digital assets from a wide range of cyber threats, including hackers, malware, ransomware, and insider threats. It involves staying up-to-date on the latest risks, solving complex puzzles, collaborating with diverse teams, and navigating ethical challenges. In contrast, working in IT focuses more on managing and maintaining technology infrastructure, systems, and applications to support an organisation's operations. While both fields intersect, cyber security typically involves a narrower focus on security measures and defence strategies against malicious activities.Understandably, you may be wondering where to begin with so many areas of cyber to venture into.
