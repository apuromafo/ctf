## Cyber security skills employers are looking for:

## **Technical skills** required for cyber security
**1. Threat Detection and Incident Response**Identifying and responding to cyber threats is critical in minimising damage and preventing breaches. Expertise in SIEM platforms, incident response playbooks, and forensic tools (e.g., [Autopsy](https://tryhackme.com/room/autopsy2ze0?ref=blog.tryhackme.com), [Volatility](https://tryhackme.com/room/volatility?ref=blog.tryhackme.com)) is essential for analysing threats and executing timely responses. A solid incident response strategy helps organisations mitigate risks and recover efficiently.**2. Network Security and Infrastructure**Securing networks is the backbone of cyber security. Professionals must understand firewall management, intrusion detection systems (IDS), and network protocols like TCP/IP, DNS, and HTTPS. Strong network security skills help organisations build resilient defences against attacks like DDoS, MITM, and ransomware.**3. Penetration Testing and Vulnerability Assessment**Ethical hacking is vital for uncovering security weaknesses before attackers do. Tools like [Metasploit](https://tryhackme.com/r/module/metasploit?ref=blog.tryhackme.com), [Burp Suite](https://tryhackme.com/module/learn-burp-suite?ref=blog.tryhackme.com), and [Nmap](https://tryhackme.com/r/room/furthernmap?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) enable professionals to simulate attacks and identify vulnerabilities!**4. Cloud Security**With the rise of [AWS](https://tryhackme.com/path/outline/attackinganddefendingaws?ref=blog.tryhackme.com), Azure, and Google Cloud, securing cloud environments is more important than ever. Employers value expertise in identity and access management (IAM), cloud security configurations, and container security to prevent misconfigurations and data breaches in cloud infrastructures.**5. Programming and Scripting**Knowledge of [programming languages](https://tryhackme.com/r/resources/blog/programming-language-for-cyber-security?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) like Python, JavaScript, and Bash is essential for automating security tasks, analysing malware, and developing security tools. Writing scripts helps streamline incident response and vulnerability scanning processes.**6. Cyber Security Frameworks and Compliance**Understanding security frameworks such as NIST, ISO 27001, and GDPR is key to meeting regulatory and organisational requirements. [Compliance expertise](https://tryhackme.com/room/cybergovernanceregulation?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) ensures businesses adhere to industry standards, reducing legal and financial risks associated with data breaches.
## **Soft skills required for cyber security**
**1. ****Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving******Cyber security is all about tackling complex challenges, from identifying security vulnerabilities to responding to cyber attacks. Strong analytical skills and the ability to think several steps ahead help professionals anticipate threats, evaluate risks, and develop innovative solutions to protect organisations from evolving cyber risks.**2. ****Communication Skills******Cyber security professionals often need to translate technical jargon into clear, actionable insights for executives, employees, and clients. The ability to explain security risks, recommend best practices, and collaborate with IT teams, legal departments, and management ensures that security strategies are effectively implemented across an organisation.**3. ****Ethics and Integrity******Handling sensitive data requires a high level of trust and ethical responsibility. Cyber security professionals must adhere to industry regulations, company policies, and ethical hacking guidelines while protecting confidential information. Maintaining integrity in security roles helps prevent insider threats and fosters a culture of security awareness.**4. ****Teamwork and Leadership******Working alongside IT teams, developers, compliance officers, and business leaders is crucial for maintaining a strong security posture. Professionals with leadership skills can guide teams during security incidents, mentor junior colleagues, and drive security initiatives that align with business objectives.
