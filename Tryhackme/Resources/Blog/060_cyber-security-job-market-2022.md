## Trends in the Cyber Security Job Market
While demand for cyber security professionals remains at an all-time high, [51% of businesses](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1072767/Cyber_security_skills_in_the_UK_labour_market_2022_-_findings_report.pdf?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) are experiencing a fundamental cyber security skills gap - with a third (33%) reporting an advanced technical skills gap. The skills gaps are most prominent in penetration testing, forensic analysis, security architecture or engineering, threat intelligence, interpreting malicious code, and user monitoring.With the increasing demand for cyber skills, it’s more important than ever that employers and cyber job applicants understand the growing need for high-level external training and continuous development. As a result of the growing need for cyber security professionals to join both private and government sectors, we have witnessed some of the fastest-growing salaries compared with other industries. When faced with hard-to-fill roles, a common approach for recruiters within the sector is increasing salaries and adapting job specifications to allow better flexibility and home working.In the UK, this has led to areas outside of London and the South East seeing higher market rate salaries due to the rapid increase of cyber security remote working jobs. In addition, the ever-evolving cyber security job market has become increasingly diverse over the last five years, with a rise of [women and ethnic minorities](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1072767/Cyber_security_skills_in_the_UK_labour_market_2022_-_findings_report.pdf?ref=blog.tryhackme.com) working in cyber security. In terms of gender and ethnicity, the overall diversity of the cyber sector is anticipated to further improve over the next decade.
